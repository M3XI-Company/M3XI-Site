Policy 02 · CallMe — meeting new people by video call

CallMe

CallMe is an app for meeting new people on live calls. A call opens on voice, and video turns on only when both people agree. Every user's age is verified before they can call anyone, and some of what CallMe uses is sensitive, so this policy is deliberately specific about what that involves.

Age and identity verification

CallMe is strictly 18+, and verification is mandatory — there is no way to skip it and reach the calling features. The check is carried out by Didit, our verification provider, and involves three steps:

  • photographing an identity document — passport, driving licence or national ID;
  • taking a live selfie, checked for liveness: that a real person is present rather than a photograph, mask or video;
  • a face match between that selfie and the photograph on your document.

The face match is biometric processing. Under UK and EU GDPR, biometric data used to identify someone is special-category data, and we rely on your explicit consent together with the substantial public interest in keeping under-18s out of an adult service.

Your document images and selfie are never sent to us and are never stored on our servers. They go directly to Didit. We receive only the outcome — pass or fail, your date of birth, and your derived age.

Data we collect

  • Account. Sign-in with Google, Apple, or an email address and password; your display name; and an internal user ID.
  • Verification outcome. Pass or fail, date of birth and derived age — not the documents themselves.
  • Profile. Age, city, bio, gender, interests, and optionally occupation, education, relationship aim, sexual orientation and ethnicity. The last two are special-category fields; ethnicity is optional throughout.
  • MeCards. The cards you design — the photograph you add, a GIF, the words you write, a colour style, and any stickers you place on them.
  • Preferences. Age range, distance, which genders you want to be matched with, and optionally which broad ethnicity groups — used only to pair you with compatible people. See "Ethnicity in matching" below.
  • Location. Your city, and precise GPS coordinates if you choose to allow location, for distance-based matching. You can type a city instead. Other people are shown your city and a distance, never your coordinates.
  • Photos. Images you upload to a MeCard, shown during call intros. If you and your match both opt in to connect, the photo captured at that moment is saved to each of your connection walls.
  • Call metadata. Who was matched with whom, call start and end times, whether video was mutually enabled, and connection quality — needed to run the service, prevent abuse and improve matching.
  • Connections and friends. When you and another person both choose to connect, each of you can see the social handle the other chose to share. We also keep the friend requests you send and accept, and your friends list.
  • Safety reports. If you report, block or mute someone, we store the report and enough context to act on it, plus any moderation flags raised against your content.
  • Safety decisions and appeals. Strikes, suspensions and bans on your account with the evidence behind them, a keyed identity fingerprint (see "Safety monitoring, strikes and bans"), and any appeal you send — your email address, what you wrote and the files you attach.
  • Invites. The one-time invite links you create, whether each was used and when, and how many friends joined; if you joined through an invite, which link and whether it qualified. When you install through an invite link, Google Play passes the code to the app.
  • Purchases. Your Premium subscription status, the coin packs you buy, and how you spend coins in the app. Card details are handled entirely by Apple or Google and never reach us.
  • Sticker searches. What you type into the sticker and GIF picker is sent to GIPHY to return results.
  • Advertising. CallMe shows ads via Google AdMob, which uses your device's advertising ID. See "Advertising & consent" below.

Your calls

  • We do not record call audio or video. No recording of a call is ever kept.
  • To keep calls safe, what is said may be turned into text while the call is live, and still frames of video may be checked for nudity. How that works, and what is kept, is set out in "Safety monitoring, strikes and bans" below. The app tells you before it applies to you.
  • Every call opens on voice. Video turns on only when both people opt in during the call, and you can turn your camera off at any time.
  • Your phone number and email are never shown to other users; calls connect through the app.

Safety monitoring, strikes and bans

CallMe uses automated safety tools on calls and at tables, because people meeting strangers deserve protection that does not depend on someone managing to report in the moment.

  • Speech. While a call is live, its audio is passed through a speech-to-text service (Agora) to produce a transcript. No audio is kept. After the call ends, the transcript is read by an AI safety reviewer (Anthropic's Claude) that looks for sexual messages someone did not want, sexual pressure or threats, threats of violence, sharing someone's private details, harassment, slurs, and anything sexual involving someone who may be under 18. Speakers are labelled anonymously for the review. Sentences that trip a list of hateful, sexual or threatening words are also read during the call, so serious harm can be stopped while it happens.
  • During a call. Hateful language that is unmistakable gets an instant in-call warning and a strike; doing it again removes and bans the person. Serious harm — unwanted sexual messages, sexual pressure, threats, sharing private details, or sexual talk involving a possible minor — removes the person at once with a short provisional ban while a person on our safety team reviews it. The live check acts only when it is certain: never on tone of voice or accent, never on quotes, song lyrics or discussion of racism, and never on words the speech recogniser may have misheard. Anything less certain waits for the full review after the call.
  • Video. When video is on, the apps of the other people on the call may send occasional still frames of your video (including a shared screen) to an image moderation service (Sightengine) to check for nudity and sexual content. A frame that looks explicit hides that video from the person watching straight away, with no penalty, and asks the person's own app for a frame of their own camera. A permanent ban needs that frame to agree (or three different people's apps at a table); if their own frame is only suspicious they are removed with a provisional ban pending review, and if it is clean nothing happens to them and a person looks. A frame is kept only when it is evidence of a breach; clean frames are not stored anywhere. Frames that may show a minor are never sent to an AI model and go straight to a person.
  • If it happens on your call. When someone on your call is warned or removed, you get a short letter saying so — without their name or details — and the two of you are never matched again. You never need to report it yourself.
  • Nobody can be framed. Every app on a call forwards what the speech-to-text service sent it, yours included, and words only count as evidence when your own app received the same words (or, at a table, several other people's did). A suspicious frame of your video asks your own app for a frame of your own camera, and a ban needs the two to agree. What one person's phone alone reports about you is never enough to sanction you; at most it is looked at by a person.
  • What can happen. The tools can give a strike, suspend an account or ban it permanently, automatically, and a person on our safety team reviews every permanent ban they make. Serious direct harm — unwanted sexual messages, nudity on camera, sexual threats, threats of violence, sharing private details, or anything sexual involving a minor — is a permanent ban. Abuse, slurs or harassment lead to a strike or a suspension of a few days to a few weeks, and a second strike within 90 days leads to a suspension. When it happens you are taken out of the call, told the reason, how long it lasts and a reference, and the app locks until the ban ends or is lifted.
  • Identity fingerprint. To stop a banned person returning with a new account, we keep a one-way keyed fingerprint (an HMAC) derived from the document details your verification returns — never the document or its number in readable form. A new account whose fingerprint matches a banned one is banned too. A looser match, on name and date of birth only, is never acted on automatically; it is flagged for a person to review. The same fingerprint stops one person claiming a friend's invite reward more than once.
  • Appeals and human review. You can appeal any decision at m3xi.com/appeal or by emailing safety@m3xi.com, with anything that shows what happened. An automated reviewer can accept an appeal; only a person on our safety team can refuse one, and they will tell you why. This is how we meet your rights under UK GDPR to human intervention in, and to contest, a decision made automatically about you.
  • Legal basis. Our legitimate interests in keeping people safe, and our duties under the Online Safety Act 2023. Suspected child sexual abuse material is reported to the National Crime Agency, as the law requires.

Photos, stickers and moderation

Images and text you publish may be scanned automatically by a content moderation provider before they appear. Content that fails is blocked and the account is flagged; repeated flags lead to a warning, then suspension, then a permanent ban. Stickers and GIFs come from GIPHY and are requested at GIPHY's family-safe "G" rating.

Ethnicity in matching

Ethnicity is optional everywhere in CallMe. You can state it on your profile, and you can separately choose broad ethnicity groups you would like to be matched with. These are two different things and we ask for each separately, because storing a characteristic and filtering people by it are different purposes under data protection law.

Ethnicity is special-category data under UK and EU GDPR. We rely on your explicit consent to use it in matching, given when you set that preference. You can clear it at any time from Settings → Who should we call?, and matching immediately stops using it.

How the preference behaves, so you know what you are agreeing to:

  • If you set no preference, it has no effect at all — you are matched with everyone.
  • Someone who has not stated their ethnicity is never excluded by anyone's preference. Choosing not to share yours does not cost you matches.
  • The preference is the first one we relax. After roughly eight seconds in the queue it stops applying, before we widen your age range or distance. It shapes who you are offered first; it is not a wall.

Gender and orientation preferences work differently — those are never relaxed.

What other people can see

  • During a call: your display name, age, city, interests and the MeCard you were dealt.
  • For 24 hours after a call, the person you spoke to may see one of your MeCards on their home screen. It disappears on its own.
  • Your social handle is revealed only when both of you have chosen to connect. A one-sided request never reveals it.
  • People you block or mute cannot be matched with you unless you undo it, and apart from your lists in Settings they do not appear in your app.

How long we keep things

  • Account, profile and MeCards — until you delete your account.
  • Recent calls list in the app — visible for 48 hours.
  • MeCard wall in the app — visible for 24 hours.
  • Call metadata — up to 12 months, so we can investigate reports.
  • Verification outcome — while your account exists; it is what keeps you verified.
  • Call transcripts — deleted as soon as they have been reviewed, normally within minutes of the call ending. Only the sentences behind a decision are kept, with that decision.
  • Evidence frames and appeal files — kept with the decision they relate to, for as long as that decision is on record.
  • Identity fingerprint — while your account exists, and after it closes, so that a ban cannot be escaped and an invite reward cannot be claimed twice by deleting an account.
  • Purchase records — kept after an account closes where we need them for tax, accounting and to stop a store transaction being credited twice.
  • Reports, the decisions made on them, and any ban — retained after an account closes, together with a minimal record of the closed account (its verification session references and any sanction on it, with no name, photo or profile), so that a banned user cannot simply come back.

Ground rules

  • 18+ only. CallMe is strictly for adults. Accounts found to belong to minors are removed.
  • We never sell your matching preferences or match history, and we never share them with advertisers. Ads in CallMe are not targeted using your profile, preferences or who you matched with.
  • Blocking someone prevents them from being matched with you again.
  • Delete your account from Settings → Delete account & data, or, if you no longer have the app installed, by emailing us as explained at m3xi.com/delete-account. We erase your profile, MeCards, photos, preferences and match history within 30 days, except the safety and purchase records described above.
  • You must not create an account for anyone else, use another person's identity document, or attempt to defeat the verification check.

Advertising & consent

Ads are served by Google AdMob. In the UK and EEA you will see a consent form when the app first opens, provided by Google's User Messaging Platform, and can change your answer any time from Settings → Ad privacy options. On iOS you will also see Apple's tracking prompt; declining still leaves every feature available. You can also choose to watch a rewarded advert to earn an extra call. As above, your matching data is never used to target ads.

Subprocessors

Supabase (auth, database, storage — EU region) · Didit (identity document, liveness and face-match verification — EU) · Agora (live voice and video transport, and live speech-to-text for safety — calls are not recorded or stored) · Anthropic (AI safety review of call transcripts and appeals — USA) · Sightengine (image and video-frame moderation — EU) · Resend (appeal emails — USA) · Google AdMob (advertising) · GIPHY (sticker and GIF search — USA) · content moderation provider (automated image and text scanning) · ElevenLabs (the synthesised host voice used in call intros) · Google Play & Apple (purchase verification) · Vercel or similar (hosting).

Where a provider processes data outside the UK or EEA, we rely on Standard Contractual Clauses or the UK International Data Transfer Addendum.

Policy 03 · M3XI Spatial — property walkthroughs for agencies

M3XI Spatial

M3XI Spatial is an online tool for estate and letting agencies. Agencies scan or photograph a property, or build a walkthrough from a floor plan, and publish it for buyers and tenants to walk through in a browser. This policy covers two groups of people: agency users, who have an account, and visitors, who open a walkthrough and may send an enquiry.

If you have an account

  • Account. Your email address, from Google Sign-In or a one-time sign-in link, and an internal user ID.
  • Profile. Optionally a username, a symbol for your avatar, an Instagram handle and a website link, shown as the maker of walkthroughs you publish.
  • Your organisation and plan. The agency you belong to, your business plan, your build-credit balance and how credits were used.
  • What you upload. Property scans, 360° photographs, room photographs, floor plans, the property's address and postcode, room names, notes you place in a walkthrough, and your agency's branding: name, colours, logo, email, phone and website. Photographs keep whatever information your camera stored in them, such as when and where they were taken.
  • What you write. Descriptions and prompts you use to build a walkthrough.
  • What we measure. When a scan is attached we calculate facts about it — which way is up, where the floor and walls are, how much can be walked, and how much the camera did not capture — and store them with the scan.

If you visit a walkthrough

  • Opening a walkthrough needs no account. We keep anonymous counts of views, not who viewed.
  • Sending an enquiry. If you use Enquire, we store your name, the email address or phone number you give, your message, and which room you were in when you sent it. We deliver it to the agency that published the walkthrough: always to their dashboard, and by email where email delivery is set up.
  • The agency decides what to do with your enquiry and is responsible for how it uses your details. We store and deliver enquiries on the agency's behalf and do not use them for anything else.

Who can see what

Files for walkthroughs built in the Spatial Engine are kept in a public file store. Anyone who has the exact address of a file can open it, including while the walkthrough is still a draft. The address is long and not published anywhere, but it is not private. Do not upload anything to the Spatial Engine that you would not be comfortable being seen.

  • Property tours made with /capture/ use private storage. Photographs and scans are only reachable through short-lived links, and a tour is not public until the agency presses Go live.
  • A published walkthrough or tour can be opened by anyone with its link and may be listed publicly.
  • A draft is shared only through a private preview link, which the agency can replace to shut off old links.

Building a walkthrough with AI

When you build a walkthrough from photographs, a floor plan or a description, the material you give is sent to the services below to do that work, and the result is stored with us. Generated walkthroughs are labelled as AI-generated wherever they appear.

  • World Labs (USA) receives your photographs and description and generates the walkable space.
  • Groq (USA) reads floor plans and room photographs to work out the rooms and how they connect, and rewrites your description if you switch on prompt sharpening.
  • OpenStreetMap Nominatim looks up the postcode and house number you give, and Google Street View or Mapillary supplies a street-level photograph of the front of the property. We keep a copy of that photograph with the walkthrough.

How long we keep things

  • Walkthroughs, tours, scans and photographs — until the agency deletes them, or closes its account.
  • Enquiries — until the agency asks us to delete them or closes its account. Deleting a tour does not delete the enquiries it received, so an agency does not lose its leads by accident.
  • Account and plan records — while the account exists, and afterwards where we need them for tax and accounting.

Payments

Business plans are arranged directly with us. If you pay by card, the payment is processed by Stripe; we receive the payment's status, never your full card number.

Subprocessors

Supabase (auth, database, storage — EU region) · Vercel (hosting) · World Labs (walkthrough generation — USA) · Groq (floor-plan reading and prompt rewriting — USA) · OpenStreetMap Nominatim (address lookup) · Google Street View and Mapillary (street photographs) · Resend (enquiry emails — USA) · Stripe (card payments) · Google (sign-in).

Policy 04 · Products on hold

Cornelia and AutoUV

We have put these two products on hold to focus on CallMe and M3XI Spatial. Neither is being sold, downloaded or signed up to. This section says what we still hold and how to have it removed.

Cornelia (NoteTaker)

Cornelia was a study note-taking app. It was never released publicly on an app store. If you joined the Cornelia waitlist, we hold your email address as described under Website & waitlists. If you used an early test version, anything it recorded or generated stayed on your phone unless you switched on sync; any account details held for testers stay in our paused database until you ask us to delete them. Email support@m3xi.com and we will remove them.

AutoUV

AutoUV is a desktop add-on for Blender. It processed your meshes, textures and UVs entirely on your own computer and never uploaded your models or scenes to us.

  • Update checks. A copy that is already installed may still contact our website to compare its version against the latest release. Like any web request, this involves your IP address.
  • Licences and trials. If you started a trial or were issued a licence key, we hold the email address and key used to validate it. Card payments were processed by Stripe, which holds the payment record; we received the payment's status, never your card number.
  • Crash reports were only ever sent if you chose to send them, and never included your mesh data.

To have your AutoUV licence details removed, or to ask about a charge, email support@m3xi.com.

Your rights & the shared small print

Legal bases (UK / EU GDPR)

Contract — delivering the products and features you ask for, including sending an enquiry to the agency you chose. Where data lives

Our primary database region is the EU (Supabase, eu-west-1). Your rights

Depending on your jurisdiction you can request access, correction, deletion, portability, restriction or objection, and you can complain to a regulator. UK users can contact the ICO at ico.org.uk. Send requests to support@m3xi.com from your registered email where possible — we respond to all of them. For an enquiry you sent through M3XI Spatial, you can also contact the agency directly.

Changes

When we update these policies we change the date at the top of this page; for material changes we notify you in the app or by email. See also our Terms.
