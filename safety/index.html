Safety on CallMe

Safety, built in.

CallMe is a place for ID-checked adults to talk face to face on video calls, one-to-one, at tables and with your crew, and to keep in touch with the people they click with. Safety is part of every call rather than a page of settings. Everyone has passed an adult ID check, video starts only when you both choose it, a new person's video is checked before you see it, nothing is shared unless you both tap Keep in touch, and reporting or blocking someone takes you straight out of the call. A person on our safety team acts on every report within 24 hours.

Who is here

Everyone is 18+ and verified.

CallMe is for adults aged 18 and over, and the age check is not a box to tick. Nobody can call anyone, sit at a table or join a crew until they have passed it.

  • An ID check, not a date of birth

    Everyone verifies their age before they can call anyone, with an identity document, a live selfie and a face match. The check is carried out by our verification provider, Didit.

  • No way round it

    There is no way to skip verification and reach the calling features, and anyone whose document shows they are under 18 cannot complete it. Nobody under 18 is ever put on a call, however long you wait.

  • We never keep your ID or selfie

    Your document images and selfie go to Didit. We receive the result: pass or fail, your date of birth and your age. For the photo check, your verification selfie passes through our server's memory to compare it with your card photo. CallMe never stores your ID images or selfie.

    The photo check runs only when you tap Verify my photo, and if it is clearly you, your card shows a Photo verified stamp.

Verification proves someone is a real adult. It is not a background check, so never share financial details or your home address.

On a call

Video by choice. Checked before you see it.

Calls on CallMe are face to face, but nobody sees you just because a call has connected.

  • Both of you choose video

    Before a one-to-one call you choose whether to start on video, and you see yourself first. The call opens on video only when both of you chose it, and either of you can stay on voice.

    At a table, everyone arrives with their microphone off and their camera off. A table where cameras are on, such as Face to Face, says so and asks before you join.

  • Checked before you see it

    A new person’s video reaches you blurred, with “Checking video…”, until our server has checked it, usually in two or three seconds. If it can’t be checked, it stays hidden and you can keep talking. The blur comes back whenever they start a new camera or turn theirs back on.

    Opening the shield blurs everyone’s video on your phone at once, and after you report someone, theirs stays hidden for the rest of the call.

  • Your camera stays yours

    One person can never switch on the other person's camera, and our servers check that both of you chose video, every time. You can turn your camera off at any time without ending the call.

    The preview of yourself before a call never leaves your phone. Your phone number and email are never shown to anyone you talk to.

While video is on, our server checks still frames for nudity, and an explicit one pauses that camera for everyone on the call. Calls are never recorded. Pressuring someone to turn on video breaks the CallMe terms.

After a call

Keep in touch, only if you both choose it.

If you get on, you can both tap Keep in touch: then you see each other’s socials and can message and call each other. Nothing is shared until you both tap.

  • One tap each, and private

    After a couple of minutes on a call, or five at a table, either of you can tap Keep in touch. Nothing is sent when you tap. If they tap it too, you’re in touch. If they don’t, your tap lapses after 48 hours, and a tap that isn’t returned is never shown to anyone.

    There is no button to keep in touch with everyone at once: every tap is for one person.

  • Before you swap socials

    The first time, CallMe shows a short safety card and asks for one quick selfie, to show it’s still you holding the phone. The card says what matters most: on other apps, people aren’t ID-checked and we can’t see what happens; never send intimate photos or money to someone you’ve just met; and if someone threatens to share pictures of you, don’t pay. Report them here, even if it happened on another app.

    StopNCII.org and the Revenge Porn Helpline can help too.

  • When it can’t happen

    Keeping in touch needs both of you to be ID-checked, in good standing and not blocked either way. It never happens for 30 days after either of you reports the other, or after a still of either of you was found explicit on a call or table you shared, and there are daily limits for everyone.

  • Your socials, your choice

    You can add up to four socials. Only people you keep in touch with see them, and in a group chat only the members who keep in touch with you. You can hide any of them from any one person, and they aren’t told.

    Handles that lead to adult-content, scam or link pages can’t be added, and nor can handles from accounts we have banned for that.

  • Group chats ask first

    From a table or a call you can start a group chat with the people who were there. Anyone you’re not in touch with gets an invite that shows who is already in it, with Join or Not now, and Not now keeps invites from that person quiet for 30 days. You choose who can invite you, in Settings, under Privacy.

  • Stop at any time

    On their page, tap Stop keeping in touch: you both stop seeing each other’s socials at once, and your messages with them close. They aren’t told. Blocking someone does all of that, both ways.

    Once someone has followed you on another app, CallMe can’t undo that, so block them there too if you need to.

Something wrong?

Get out first. We deal with the rest.

Tap the shield in the top corner of a call. When you report or block someone, the app mutes your microphone and takes you out of the call without waiting for the report to send, so a bad connection can never keep you there. If sending fails, the app tries again later.

Behind the shield

  • Silence someone, just for you They stay on the call and never know. You stay on it too, and you can undo it at any time.
  • Someone sounds underage This has its own option, so it is never buried in a list of reasons. You leave the call, and your report goes to the front of our review queue straight away.
  • Report someone Pick what happened: sexual content or exposure, threats or danger, threatening to share pictures of you, harassment or bullying, exploitation or trafficking, hate or abuse, asking you for money, scam or spam, a social that isn’t theirs or leads somewhere harmful, or something else. You leave the call, and you will not be put on a call with them again.
  • Not sure who? Flag the call Nobody gets singled out. Our team looks at the whole call.
  • Block someone You leave the call, and neither of you can be put on a call with the other again, or join a table or call the other is in. Any friendship between you ends. On a group call, the people who came with them are blocked too. You can unblock someone later in Settings.
  • Leave straight away Tap Leave the call in the shield, or press and hold the shield, to leave at once, with nothing to choose and nothing to confirm. Ending a call always works too.

  • After a call

    The call summary has a link, Something wasn’t right on this call, that opens the same options. There you can also choose not to be put on a call with someone again, without reporting them, and change your mind later in Settings.

    For 48 hours after a call or a table, you can also report anyone you were with from People you talked to, even after you have both left. Blocking someone never stops you reporting them.

  • At a table

    When you are sitting with a few people, you can silence someone, block someone, or flag the whole table so our team looks at it.

  • In a chat

    Press and hold a message to report it, or block the person who sent it. A report keeps a copy of the message, so deleting it afterwards does not hide it from us.

  • Posters, snapshots and profiles

    Every poster, snapshot, card design and profile can be reported from where you see it, and you can block the person behind it from the same place.

  • Hide it

    Hide a poster, snapshot, table or message and it disappears for you straight away. You do not have to report it first, and you can still report it if it breaks the rules.

  • Your lists, in Settings

    Settings shows who you have blocked, who you have chosen not to be put on a call with, what you have hidden, and your reports.

What we do with reports

What happens after a report.

You will not be put on a call with the person again, and that part happens straight away. Within 24 hours, a person decides the rest.

  • A person reviews it within 24 hours

    A person on our safety team reviews every report within 24 hours. If it broke the rules, we remove what was posted and remove the account that posted it from CallMe.

    You get a reference, and you can see it and where it has got to in Settings, under Your reports.

  • Serious reports come first

    A report that someone sounds underage, of exploitation or trafficking, of sexual content, of threats, or of someone threatening to share pictures of you can pause the other account while we check. An account reported as underage can be kept out of calls until it passes a fresh ID check, and it can’t keep in touch with anyone until it has been checked again.

  • Nobody is told who reported them

    A report never tells the other person who filed it. It does not tell you what happened to their account either.

    If you were in touch with them, your socials are hidden from them at once, and you can stop keeping in touch or block them from the same place.

  • Repeated reports add up

    Reports from separate calls lead to a warning, then to a suspension, and the suspensions grow longer if it keeps happening.

  • We can go further

    We may warn, suspend or permanently ban an account, remove content, or refer a matter to the police. We may act on a single serious report without warning.

  • The record stays

    Reports, and the decisions made on them, are kept after an account closes, so deleting an account does not wipe the record of what someone did.

Crews, friends and walks

Only friends, only if you say so.

Your crew is for friends only. The friends map, snapshots and dog walks each start off, or private to you, until you choose otherwise.

  • Nobody moves without their own Ready

    Your crew is you and up to three friends. Only a friend can ask you into a crew, and an invite always asks. Each person taps Ready for themselves, and nobody is taken anywhere until they have. A crew link works only for the host's friends.

  • Who's around, and your door sign

    Home shows roughly how many people are around, but not who they are. Only your friends can see you there by name, and only if you have turned on Show friends what I’m up to. Only they see your door sign: Up for a chat, Just listening or Not now. It comes down by itself after 12 hours.

  • The map is off until you choose

    Location sharing starts off. Once you turn it on, nobody you haven't added as a friend can see where you are, and with Choose friends, only the ones you tick. It starts at your town, never your door, and you can switch to precise in Settings, under Location.

  • Only while CallMe is open, for a day

    Your phone sends your place only while the app is open, never in the background. Sharing switches itself off 24 hours after you turn it on, so it is only ever on because you turned it on in the last 24 hours. You can turn it off sooner in Settings, under Location.

  • Snapshots are checked first

    A snapshot is a photo for your friends that lasts 24 hours. Every one is checked before your friends see it, you can delete yours at any time, and any snapshot can be reported.

  • Your walks are yours

    A walk measures distance and pace while CallMe is open, and keeps only the name of the district it started and ended in, never a street. Your walks and bests are private, and each one can be deleted. While a walk is running, and only if you have turned on Show friends what I’m up to, your friends can see that you are out walking.

The rules

What nobody may do on CallMe.

CallMe has zero tolerance for objectionable content and for abusive users. These rules are part of the CallMe terms, which everyone agrees to in the app before they start. On a call, or anywhere in the app, you must not:

  • post, show or send sexual content or nudity, or expose yourself;
  • harass, bully, threaten, degrade or sexually harass anyone;
  • post hateful content or slurs, or attack anyone for who they are;
  • threaten or encourage violence, or show violent content;
  • bring anyone under 18 onto a call or into the app, or post anything that sexualises, exploits or endangers a child;
  • traffic, sell or exploit anyone, for sex, for work or for money, or offer, arrange or advertise anything of the kind;
  • scam anyone, or ask for money, gifts, investments or personal financial details;
  • record, screenshot or share a call or another person's images without their consent;
  • misrepresent your age or identity, or use another person's identity document;
  • promote, arrange or facilitate anything illegal;
  • pressure someone to turn on video;
  • contact someone who has blocked you, or create a new account to get round a block or ban.

The full wording is in the CallMe terms.

The small print

Your data, children, and leaving.

  • Your data

    CallMe does not record the audio or video of your calls, never turns what is said into text, and nothing listens in.

    What you write for other people is checked against CallMe's own lists of words and patterns, and no AI reads it. When video is on, still frames are checked for nudity. The preview of yourself before a call never leaves your phone.

    What we do collect, why, and how long it is kept is set out in the CallMe privacy policy.

  • Children

    CallMe is strictly for adults. If we learn that an account belongs to someone under 18, we close it and delete its data.

    Our child safety standards explain how we keep under-18s out, and how anyone can report a concern.

  • Deleting your account

    In the app, go to Settings, then Delete account and data.

    If you no longer have the app, m3xi.com/delete-account explains how to ask us, and what we keep.
